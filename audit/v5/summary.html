Serious problems by theme

Who is host

  • The first person into an empty studio becomes host, so an early guest takes the seat.
  • If the host reloads or drops for 20 s, a guest is promoted and the host returns as a guest.
  • When the host leaves, a guest silently gets the full host interface.

lib/studio.js:306–321 · public/app.js:627

Going live

  • The stream-key field clears while typing whenever someone joins or leaves.
  • Errors appear behind the Go live dialog; a failed destination is still reported as live.
  • "Connecting…" hangs forever if the relay doesn't answer, likely on Render's free plan while it wakes.
  • The watch page can show "Live" forever after the host leaves.

public/app.js:3176

Recording and consent

  • Guests get a 3-second notice, then no recording indicator.
  • The host's name tag reads "Alex Host (you)" in the recording and stream.
  • Banners cover the guest's camera in Spotlight.
  • Recorded files have no duration, so they can't be seeked.

public/app.js:650, 876 · public/layout.js

Connection and audio

  • An invalid TURN entry silently breaks all video for that person.
  • Guest audio can start blocked until someone clicks the stage.
  • Blur and replace drop to about 2 fps without a GPU, with no fallback.
  • Each person polls the server 45–60 times a minute: about 12–14k Vercel calls for a 4-person, 1-hour show.

public/fx.js:461 · public/app.js:747

Security

  1. P1
    Invite emails can be used to send spamSign-up doesn't verify email, and each scheduled show can email 20 addresses with no limit on shows.
  2. P2
    Password-reset links can be pointed at another siteThe link is built from the request's Host header when APP_URL is unset. Set APP_URL in Vercel now.
  3. P2
    Account takeover through Google sign-in linkingSomeone can register your email first, then be linked when you sign in with Google.
  4. P2
    The free AI allowance can be drainedNo account needed, and the limit trusts a spoofable X-Forwarded-For header.
  5. P2
    Knock and relay-slot flooding; account lockout300 anonymous knocks were all accepted; one relay token can fill every relay slot; 11 wrong passwords lock anyone out of any account.
  6. OK
    What held upGuests were refused every host-only action. Chat and banner XSS attempts were inert. Stream keys never reached gather's server or the relay logs, and the relay rejected missing, forged and expired tokens.

What works

Video calls connect for every participant, with roughly 10 MB of memory per person. All 11 landscape and 8 vertical scenes render. Screen share, recording, invite links, backstage, removing guests, the 10-step tour, sign-up and sign-in, and every public link work. Going live was verified end to end: a local copy of the relay sent real H.264 and AAC to an RTMP receiver. Landing pages load in about half a second with almost no layout shift.

Tests: 9 of 13 suites passed on the first run. The other four failed because of the sandbox (one missing package, three timeouts while four auditors shared the machine) and pass alone. Separately, the feat suite fails intermittently in CI and should be investigated.

Making it look better

Today

  • The video covers 43% of a laptop screen and 24% of a phone. StreamYard gives it 55–60%.
  • 25 pieces of text are 9–11 px; phone name tags draw at about 6 px.
  • Twelve scene tiles scroll sideways and get cut off; Settings is one sheet of about 60 controls.
  • On phones, the recording pill pushes Go live off the screen.

Quick wins (about a week)

  • Stage first: drop the frame, collapsible panel, size from screen height. Target 65% of the work area.
  • One type scale: 12 px minimum, 14 px controls, 16 px inputs.
  • A vertical scene rail with six thumbnails and "More".
  • Red only for recording and live, shown to everyone.
  • A 44 px phone header and bottom bar; Settings in four sections.

Go live without stream keys

Sign in to YouTube, Twitch or Facebook once, then go live in 3 clicks. A 10-second test stream runs before the first show.

Recordings that can't be lost

Each person records locally and uploads every 5 seconds, which also gives separate tracks per person. Sam, the AI producer, makes a transcript and three vertical clips afterwards.

A run of show that runs the show

An ordered list of segments drives scenes, media and banners. Prepare mode shows the panels; On-air mode gives the stage about 65% and Space moves to the next segment.
